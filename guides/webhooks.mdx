Outbound webhooks deliver signed lifecycle notifications to your HTTPS
endpoint: mandate approvals and revocations, authorization outcomes, and the
rest of the journal. A notification is a trigger, never the proof. It tells
you something happened; you confirm what happened by fetching current state
and the evidence bundle.

## Register an endpoint

```
POST   /v1/webhook-endpoints                  register (secret shown once)
GET    /v1/webhook-endpoints                  list (never secrets)
DELETE /v1/webhook-endpoints/{id}             soft delete
PUT    /v1/webhook-endpoints/{id}/reactivation   disabled -> active
PUT    /v1/webhook-endpoints/{id}/secret      rotate the signing secret
```

Up to five non-deleted endpoints per organization. The signing secret is 32
random bytes, presented exactly once as `att_wh_<base64url>`. Store it
server-side. After a secret rotation, retries and new deliveries are signed
with the new secret; update your receiver promptly, and note that failed
verifications count toward the endpoint's failure budget.

## The delivery

```
POST <your endpoint>
Content-Type: application/json
Attesso-Webhook-Version: 1
Attesso-Signature: t=1792238400,v1=9f8e...  (hex HMAC-SHA256)
User-Agent: attesso-webhooks/1

{"id":"evt_...","type":"mandate.approved","occurred_at":"2026-10-16T12:00:00.123456789Z",
 "data":{"mandate_id":"mnd_...","state":"ACTIVE","version":2}}
```

The envelope carries the event id, its type, the occurrence time, and the
cross-references (`mandate_id`, `authorization_id` when the event belongs to
one, `state` when it names one, `version`). Events that name a state carry
it; `authorization.committed` and its terminal siblings do not. Journal
payload contents never cross. Respond 2xx; anything else is a failure and
the delivery retries.

## Verify every delivery

1. Parse `t` and `v1` from `Attesso-Signature`. `t` is a canonical base-10
   integer in seconds; `v1` is exactly 64 hex characters.
2. Check `t` is within 300 seconds of your clock (replay window).
3. Decode the presented secret: strip the `att_wh_` prefix, base64url-decode
   the remainder to its 32 raw bytes, and recompute HMAC-SHA256 over the
   string `t + "." + body` with those raw bytes. Compare in constant time
   against `v1`.

Verify the exact raw body bytes. A re-serialization changes the bytes and
breaks the signature. Runnable verifiers with shared vectors:

```bash
curl -O https://raw.githubusercontent.com/douwe-kramer/docs/main/examples/verify-webhook.js
curl -O https://raw.githubusercontent.com/douwe-kramer/docs/main/examples/verify-webhook-vectors.json
node verify-webhook.js verify-webhook-vectors.json
```

## Retries and disabling

Failures retry with exponential backoff starting at 30 seconds (plus or
minus 20 percent, about nine attempts over roughly 21 hours). After the
window the delivery fails terminally and the endpoint's exhausted-delivery
counter grows. An endpoint that fails 20 attempts in a row or exhausts 10
deliveries in a row is disabled automatically; reactivate it (counters
reset) once it is healthy again. Deliveries may arrive more than once:
deduplicate on the event id. A disabled endpoint's pending deliveries are
frozen and resume on reactivation; a deleted endpoint keeps its history
with those deliveries recorded and never retried.

## Then fetch state

The notification is not the proof. After verifying the signature, fetch the
current mandate state and, when you need a durable artifact, the signed
evidence bundle (`GET /v1/mandates/{mandate_id}/evidence`); confirm the
event carries the version you acted on. Delivery stops if the worker is
stopped; state and evidence are the source of truth either way.
